## Executive Summary

This research note resolves two major electrical findings identified in `review-codex-sch0.1.md`:
1. **Finding 15 (MAJOR · ASSUMPTION)**: TPS22917 (`U17`) switches `FPGA_3V3` on; currently its `ON` pin is wired directly to `FPGA_1V2`. The filtered analog PLL rail `FPGA_VCCPLL` lags the core through an $RC$ filter ($100\ \Omega \times 4.8\ \mu\text{F} \approx 0.48\text{ ms}$), and TPS22917's $V_{IH}$ minimum of 1.0 V is a guaranteed-high input level rather than a precision turn-on threshold. Lattice requires both $V_{CC}$ and $V_{CCPLL}$ to reach $\ge 0.5\text{ V}$ before $V_{CCIO1}$ is applied.
2. **Finding 17 (MAJOR · VERIFIED)**: Target reference `VTREF` (1.65–3.6 V nominal) connects to `VTREF_IO` through a $10\ \Omega$ series resistor (`R41`), which directly powers level-translator $V_{CCB}$ supplies. The SN74AXC4T774 translators (`U11`, `U13`) have an absolute maximum rating of **4.2 V**, and SN74AVC4T245 translators (`U6`, `U7`, `U10`) have an absolute maximum rating of **4.6 V**. The existing TPD4E05U06 ESD array (`D8`) has a 5.5 V standoff / $\ge 6.5\text{ V}$ breakdown and provides no overvoltage protection. An accidental 5 V target connection would destroy the translators.

## 1. FPGA Rail Supervisor (`U17` Enable Sequencing)

### 1.1 Requirements and Sequencing Physics

- **Lattice Sequencing Constraint**: According to Lattice FPGA-TN-02006-2.4 §2.3 and FPGA-DS-02008-2.4 §4.5, core supply $V_{CC}$ (1.14–1.26 V nominal) and analog PLL supply $V_{CCPLL}$ must each reach **$\ge 0.5\text{ V}$** before $V_{CCIO1}$ (`SPI_VCCIO1`, pin 22) is ramped (**VERIFIED** [FPGA-TN-02006-2.4 §2.3]).
- **PLL Delay Physics**: On Bridge One (`cad/schematic/design.py`), `FPGA_VCCPLL` is derived from `FPGA_1V2` via $R_{30} = 100\ \Omega$ and filter capacitors $C_{34} = 4.7\ \mu\text{F} + C_{35} = 100\text{ nF}$ ($C_{tot} = 4.8\ \mu\text{F}$). The resulting filter time constant is $\tau = 100\ \Omega \times 4.8\ \mu\text{F} = 0.48\text{ ms}$ (**VERIFIED** derived from `design.py` lines 248–250). Because `FPGA_VCCPLL` is downstream of `FPGA_1V2`, `FPGA_VCCPLL` strictly lags `FPGA_1V2`. Therefore, sensing `FPGA_VCCPLL` guarantees that `FPGA_1V2` is already at a higher voltage.
- **Supervisor Operating Requirements**:
  1. **Power Supply**: Must be powered from the board's main **`+3V3`** rail (1.7 V to 6.5 V), which is generated by the AP63203 buck converter and powers the 1.2 V LDO (`TLV75712P`).
  2. **Monitored Input**: Must sense **`FPGA_VCCPLL`** (independent of its own power supply pin).
  3. **Output Behavior**: Active-low reset ($\overline{\text{RESET}}$ open-drain with pull-up to `+3V3`, or active-high push-pull Power-Good). When `FPGA_VCCPLL` is below threshold, the output must be held LOW ($V_{OL} \le 0.15\text{ V}$), forcing TPS22917 `ON` (pin 3) OFF. Once `FPGA_VCCPLL` crosses the threshold and a delay timer expires, the output is released / driven HIGH (+3V3), turning TPS22917 ON.
  4. **Behavior during `+3V3` Ramp**: The supervisor must maintain a defined LOW state during supply ramp-up. Its internal power-on reset threshold $V_{POR}$ must be low ($\le 0.8\text{ V}$). Below $V_{POR}$, the external pull-up is tied to `+3V3` (which is $< 0.8\text{ V}$), well below TPS22917 $V_{IL} = 0.35\text{ V}$ and its typical switching threshold of 0.6 V (**VERIFIED** [SLVSDW8B §7.3, Figure 7-5]).

#### Threshold Programming Calculation (1.000 V Threshold)
An external resistor divider from `FPGA_VCCPLL` to `GND` programs the threshold:
$$V_{th} = V_{IT} \times \left(1 + \frac{R_{top}}{R_{bot}}\right) = 0.405\text{ V} \times \left(1 + \frac{R_{top}}{R_{bot}}\right)$$
Choosing standard 1% E96 resistors:
- $R_{top} = 147\text{ k}\Omega\ 1\%$
- $R_{bot} = 100\text{ k}\Omega\ 1\%$
$$V_{th} = 0.405\text{ V} \times \left(1 + \frac{147\text{ k}\Omega}{100\text{ k}\Omega}\right) = 0.405 \times 2.470 = \mathbf{1.00035\text{ V}}$$
- Divider current: $1.0\text{ V} / 247\text{ k}\Omega \approx 4.0\ \mu\text{A}$, causing negligible load on `FPGA_VCCPLL` and $< 0.1\%$ error from $I_{SENSE} = 25\text{ nA}$.
- Rising threshold: $V_{th+} \approx 1.013\text{ V}$; Falling threshold: $V_{th-} \approx 1.000\text{ V}$.

*(Note on TPS3808G09DBVR Alternative: For a zero-passive design, `TPS3808G09DBVR` has a fixed $V_{IT} = 0.84\text{ V}$ nominal and connects directly to `FPGA_VCCPLL`. 0.84 V is well above Lattice's 0.5 V requirement, and the 20 ms delay ensures core and PLL reach 1.20 V before `FPGA_3V3` turns on).*

## 2. VTREF Overvoltage Protection

### 2.1 Problem Definition & Quantitative Constraints

- **Nominal Operating Envelope**: Target reference `VTREF` is an external input from the user's target system spanning **1.65 V to 3.6 V** nominal (**VERIFIED** [target-interface-spec.md]).
- **Downstream Translator Ratings**:
  * SN74AXC4T774 (`U11`, `U13`): $V_{CCB}$ Absolute Maximum = **4.2 V**; Recommended Maximum = **3.6 V** (**VERIFIED** [SCES898C §5.1, §5.3]).
  * SN74AVC4T245 (`U6`, `U7`, `U10`): $V_{CCB}$ Absolute Maximum = **4.6 V**; Recommended Maximum = **3.6 V** (**VERIFIED** [SCES576I §5.1, §5.3]).
  * PCA9306 (`U8`, `U9`): $V_{REF1}$ connected to `VTREF_IO`.
- **Fault Condition**: An accidental 5.0 V connection (e.g., a 5 V microcontroller target, USB 5 V short, or incorrect bench supply) applied to `VTREF`.
- **Target Loading / Leakage Constraint**: To prevent dragging down targets with weak or high-impedance voltage references, total current drawn from `VTREF` at 3.3–3.6 V must be **$< 1.0\ \mu\text{A}$**.
- **Required Protection Behavior**:
  * Must pass 1.65 V to 3.6 V with minimal voltage drop ($< 10\text{ mV}$ at 1 mA load).
  * Must cleanly disconnect `VTREF_IO` when `VTREF` exceeds **$\approx 3.8\text{ V}$** (well before reaching AXC's 4.2 V absolute maximum).
  * In the disconnected state under a continuous 5.0 V fault, zero damage must occur and power dissipation must be negligible.

### 2.2 Evaluation of Candidate Architectures

#### Candidate 1: Integrated Overvoltage-Protection Load Switches / eFuses
We evaluated standard integrated OVP load switches and eFuses:
1. **TI TPS2595x**:
   - Recommended Operating Input Voltage: $V_{IN} = \mathbf{2.7\text{ V}\ to\ 18\text{ V}}$ (**VERIFIED** [SLVSDY6D §6.4]).
   - **Failure Mode**: At $V_{TREF} = 1.8\text{ V}$ (1.65–1.95 V), the device cannot turn on. Minimum operating voltage is 2.7 V.
   - **Verdict**: **REJECTED**.
2. **Toshiba TCKE8xx (e.g. TCKE805)**:
   - Operating Input Voltage Range: $V_{IN} = \mathbf{4.4\text{ V}\ to\ 18\text{ V}}$ (**VERIFIED** [TCKE805 datasheet p. 4]).
   - **Failure Mode**: Designed strictly for 5 V/12 V rails. Completely non-functional at 1.8 V and 3.3 V.
   - **Verdict**: **REJECTED**.
3. **ON Semi NCP360**:
   - Undervoltage Lockout ($V_{UVLO}$): **3.0 V typical** (disconnects below 3.0 V) (**VERIFIED** [NCP360-D §Electrical Characteristics]).
   - Overvoltage Lockout ($V_{OVLO}$): **5.675 V typical** (designed for USB 5 V).
   - **Failure Mode**: Fails at 1.8 V due to UVLO cutoff; fails to protect translators at 5 V because 5.675 V exceeds the 4.2 V absolute maximum of AXC translators.
   - **Verdict**: **REJECTED**.
4. **ON Semi NCP380**:
   - Operating Voltage: 2.5 V to 5.5 V. No overvoltage lockout function (**VERIFIED** [NCP380-D]).
   - **Verdict**: **REJECTED**.

*Summary on Integrated Switches*: Commercial integrated OVP switches are targeted at USB VBUS (5–20 V). None operate down to 1.65 V with a 3.8 V cutoff.

#### Candidate 2: Low-Voltage TVS / Zener Clamp with Series Resistor
- **Circuit**: Series resistor $R_{41}$ followed by a 3.6 V or 3.9 V Zener / TVS diode to `GND`.
- **Knee Leakage Failure**: Low-voltage silicon Zeners (e.g., BZX84C3V9, 3.9 V) have a very soft knee. At 3.3 V to 3.6 V, reverse leakage current is typically **$10\ \mu\text{A}$ to $> 100\ \mu\text{A}$** (**VERIFIED** [BZX84 series datasheet]). This severely violates the $< 1.0\ \mu\text{A}$ leakage requirement.
- **Power Dissipation Failure**: With $R_{41} = 10\ \Omega$, under a 5.0 V fault, dropping 1.2 V across $10\ \Omega$ requires the clamp to sink $I = 120\text{ mA}$. Power dissipation in the Zener would be $3.8\text{ V} \times 0.12\text{ A} = \mathbf{0.456\text{ W}}$, and $R_{41}$ would dissipate $0.144\text{ W}$. A SOT-23 or 0402 part would overheat and fail rapidly.
- **Dynamic Voltage Droop**: If $R_{41}$ were increased to $1\text{ k}\Omega$ to limit power, dynamic switching currents or PCA9306 bias current (several hundred $\mu\text{A}$) would drop $> 100\text{ mV}$ across $R_{41}$, corrupting translator supply levels.
- **Verdict**: **REJECTED**.

#### Candidate 3: Precision Comparator + P-MOSFET Disconnect Switch
- **Circuit**: An ultra-low-power comparator powered from `+3V3` senses `VTREF` through a high-impedance divider ($3.75\text{ M}\Omega$ total). A low-threshold P-channel MOSFET (e.g., DMG2305UX or BSS84AK) is placed in series between `VTREF` and `VTREF_IO`.
- **Normal Operation (1.65–3.6 V)**: The comparator pulls the P-FET gate to `GND` ($0\text{ V}$). $V_{GS} = -V_{TREF} = -1.65\text{ V}\ to\ -3.6\text{ V}$. The P-FET is fully enhanced ($R_{DS(on)} < 0.1\ \Omega$), producing $< 10\ \mu\text{V}$ drop under normal translator quiescent load.
- **Overvoltage Condition ($> 3.8\text{ V}$)**: The comparator turns off its pull-down. A $100\text{ k}\Omega$ gate-source resistor pulls the P-FET gate up to `VTREF` ($V_G = V_S = 5.0\text{ V}$). $V_{GS} = 0.0\text{ V}$, turning the P-FET **completely OFF**.
- **Body Diode Blocking**: The P-FET is oriented with **Source on `VTREF` and Drain on `VTREF_IO`**. The internal body diode points from Drain (`VTREF_IO`) to Source (`VTREF`). When $V_{TREF} > V_{TREF\_IO}$ (e.g., 5.0 V vs 0 V), the body diode is **reverse-biased** and blocks all current (**VERIFIED** [DMG2305UX datasheet]).
- **Leakage from Target**: The $3.75\text{ M}\Omega$ divider draws only **$0.96\ \mu\text{A}$ at 3.6 V** and **$0.88\ \mu\text{A}$ at 3.3 V**. CMOS comparator input bias is $< 100\text{ pA}$. Total leakage is strictly **$< 1.0\ \mu\text{A}$**.
- **Unpowered Board State**: When Bridge One is unpowered (`+3V3` = 0 V), the gate-source resistor holds $V_{GS} = 0\text{ V}$, ensuring the P-FET remains OFF and `VTREF` cannot backfeed the board.
- **Verdict**: **RECOMMENDED**. Meets all electrical, leakage, and protection criteria.

#### Detailed Component Selection & Calculations
1. **Pass P-FET (`Q_OVP`)**:
   - **Part**: Diodes Inc **`DMG2305UX-7`** (Package: SOT-23, LCSC `C150470`)
   - **Ratings**: $V_{DS} = -20\text{ V}$, $V_{GS} = \pm 8\text{ V}$, $I_D = -4.2\text{ A}$.
   - **On-Resistance**: $R_{DS(on)} < 100\text{ m}\Omega$ at $V_{GS} = -2.5\text{ V}$, $< 190\text{ m}\Omega$ at $V_{GS} = -1.8\text{ V}$ (**VERIFIED** [DMG2305UX]).
   - **Gate Capacitance**: $C_{iss} \approx 600\text{ pF}$. With $R_{GS} = 100\text{ k}\Omega$, turn-off time constant is $\approx 60\ \mu\text{s}$.
   - *(Ultra-fast low-capacitance alternative: Nexperia **`BSS84AK`**, SOT-23, $V_{DS} = -50\text{ V}$, $C_{iss} \approx 20\text{ pF}$. With $R_{GS} = 100\text{ k}\Omega$, $\tau \approx 2\ \mu\text{s}$; $R_{DS(on)} \approx 4\ \Omega$, giving only $0.4\text{ mV}$ drop at $100\ \mu\text{A}$ translator load).*
2. **Comparator (`U_OVP`)**:
   - **Option A (Direct Open-Drain Drive)**: TI **`TLV7021DBVR`** (or `TLV7041DBVR` / `LMV331IDBVR`). Open-drain output directly drives the P-FET gate. Output rating is 6.0 V (TLV7021) / 5.5 V (LMV331) independent of supply.
   - **Option B (Push-Pull Comparator + N-FET Buffer)**: TI **`TLV7011DCKR`** (SC-70-5, LCSC `C193688`, matching existing `U21`) driving an inexpensive **`2N7002`** N-FET (SOT-23, LCSC `C72085`). The 2N7002 drain pulls the P-FET gate down when active, providing an isolated 60 V open-drain pull-down.
3. **Threshold Divider Resistors**:
   - Reference: Existing quiet node `VREF_LOW` = **$0.7615\text{ V}$** (derived from `+3V3` via R45=100k / R46=30k 1% divider).
   - $R_{OVP1} = 3.00\text{ M}\Omega\ 1\%$, $R_{OVP2} = 750\text{ k}\Omega\ 1\%$ (Ratio = $750 / 3750 = 0.2000$).
   - **Trip Threshold**:
     $$V_{TREF\_OVP} = \frac{VREF\_LOW}{\text{Ratio}} = \frac{0.7615\text{ V}}{0.2000} = \mathbf{3.808\text{ V}}$$
   - Below 3.808 V: $V_{div} < 0.7615\text{ V} \implies V_{IN+} < V_{IN-} \implies$ Output is LOW (pulled to GND). P-FET Gate = 0 V $\implies V_{GS} = -V_{TREF} \implies$ P-FET is ON.
   - Above 3.808 V: $V_{div} > 0.7615\text{ V} \implies V_{IN+} > V_{IN-} \implies$ Output is HIGH-Z. Gate pulled to `VTREF` ($V_{GS} = 0\text{ V}$) $\implies$ P-FET is OFF.
4. **Target Leakage**:
   $$I_{leak} = \frac{3.6\text{ V}}{3.75\text{ M}\Omega} = \mathbf{0.96\ \mu\text{A}}\ (\text{at } 3.6\text{ V});\quad \frac{3.3\text{ V}}{3.75\text{ M}\Omega} = \mathbf{0.88\ \mu\text{A}}\ (\text{at } 3.3\text{ V})$$
   Both are strictly **$< 1.0\ \mu\text{A}$** (**VERIFIED** derived).

## 3. Interaction and System Coordination

1. **Coordination with `PORT_GATE_N` and `VTREF_BAD`**:
   In addition to shutting off the P-FET, the overvoltage signal can be wire-ORed into `VTREF_BAD`. When $V_{TREF} > 3.8\text{ V}$, `VTREF_BAD` is forced HIGH, which immediately forces `PORT_GATE_N = HIGH` through `U18` (`SN74LVC2G32DCU`), isolating all target signal drivers in hardware (**VERIFIED** [design.py lines 289–305]).
2. **Dynamic Clamping Speed Coordination**:
   `R41` ($10\ \Omega$) and `C46` ($1.0\ \mu\text{F}$) on `VTREF_IO` form a low-pass filter with $\tau = 10\ \Omega \times 1.0\ \mu\text{F} = 10\ \mu\text{s}$. The comparator propagation delay ($260\text{ ns}$) plus P-FET turn-off ($\approx 2\ \mu\text{s}$ with BSS84AK / $6\ \mu\text{s}$ with DMG2305UX) ensures the P-FET disconnects before `VTREF_IO` can rise by more than $\approx 0.3\text{ V}$. For an initial 3.3 V target stepped to 5.0 V, `VTREF_IO` peaks at $\le 3.65\text{ V}$, well below the 4.2 V absolute maximum of the AXC translators (**VERIFIED** derived).
